Tery is our Jack Russell. He is five months old, weighs 6.3 kg, and he barks. Not all the time, and not at nothing: when Tery barks, there is always someone or something out there. Most of the time it is a person walking past our outer fence along the road.

This article is about that barking: what sets him off, what we do when it happens, and why we have not tried to stop it. It is not a training programme and not a lab study, just what one young terrier does at one fence, and how we handle it.

Why Jack Russells bark

Jack Russells were bred to work: to find, chase and go after quarry, and to make noise about it. A dog like that notices everything around him, and barking is one of the ways he reports it. A terrier that barks is not broken. The useful question is not "how do I make him stop barking" but "what is he barking at, and does he stop when it is over?"

For Tery, the answer to the first part turned out to be very consistent.

What Tery barks at

  • People walking past the outer fence. This is most of it. The fence runs along the road, and when someone walks by, he goes to the fence and barks. He is guarding the house.
  • The doorbell, and loud sounds from outside. Inside the house he only barks when the doorbell rings or when something outside is loud.
  • Thunder and wind. Storms and strong wind get barked at too.

What is not on the list matters just as much: he does not bark out of boredom, and he does not bark at an empty yard. Every time we have gone to look, there was a reason.

He can't see through the fence

Our outer fence is solid, so Tery cannot see the road or the people on it. He is reacting to what he hears: steps, voices, someone passing on the other side. That is worth knowing if your terrier barks at a fence too. You cannot always remove the trigger, because a fence that blocks the view does not block the sound.

He has a big yard and a big terrace, and he is outside whenever he wants to be. So the fence is part of his territory every day, not somewhere he gets taken once in a while.

What we do when he barks

Our routine is short:

  1. We let him bark. We do not shout at him and we do not rush to stop him. He is doing the job he was bred for.
  2. He barks until the person has gone. Once the steps and voices on the other side of the fence fade, the reason is gone too.
  3. We call him. When the passer-by has gone, we call him back to us.
  4. He settles by himself. He comes back calm, without us having to calm him down.
  5. He gets a reward for guarding the house. A treat when he comes back, for doing his job and then letting it go.

Notice what the treat follows: coming back and settling, after the passer-by is gone. It does not interrupt the barking. Tery's barking has a clear end, and the reward sits right at that end.

When barking is a problem, and when it isn't

Everything above is our experience with one dog. The points below are general, and they are the ones we would check if Tery's barking ever changed:

  • It has a reason and it ends. Barking at a passer-by that stops when the person has gone is a dog doing what dogs do. That is where Tery is.
  • It does not end. A dog that keeps barking long after the trigger has gone, or barks at nothing you can find, is worth a closer look.
  • It changes suddenly. A quiet dog that starts barking a lot, or a dog that barks and shows signs of pain, is a reason to talk to a vet before assuming it is a training issue.
  • It comes with fear. Barking at thunder can be alerting, but some dogs are genuinely frightened of storms. Trembling, hiding, panting or trying to escape are signs of fear, and a fearful dog needs reassurance and a safe place more than a recall command.
  • It bothers the neighbours. Even reasonable barking can be a problem for the people living next to you. That is a fair reason to work on it, whatever the dog's motives.

If your terrier does come back when called, you already have the most useful tool. Conclusion

Tery barks at people walking past the fence, at the doorbell, at loud sounds outside, at thunder and at wind. He never barks without a reason. We let him do it, he stops when the passer-by is gone, we call him, he settles by himself and gets a reward for guarding the house. For a five-month-old terrier with a big yard, that is not a barking problem. We will update this page as he grows up and if anything changes.

FAQ

Why does my Jack Russell bark at people walking past?

Jack Russells are alert, territorial working terriers, and barking at someone near the boundary is a way of guarding the house. Our Jack Russell barks at people walking past our outer fence even though he cannot see through it, so he reacts to what he hears. He stops once the person has gone.

Should I stop my Jack Russell from barking at the fence?

It depends on whether the barking ends. If your dog barks at a real trigger and stops once it is gone, it is normal guarding. We let our terrier bark, call him once the passer-by has gone, and reward him when he comes back. Barking that never ends, starts suddenly, or comes with signs of fear or pain is worth a closer look.

Why does my Jack Russell bark at thunder and wind?

Thunder and strong wind are loud, sudden noises, and an alert terrier will often bark at them. Our Jack Russell barks at both. If your dog also trembles, hides, pants or tries to escape during storms, the barking may be fear rather than alerting, and a safe, quiet place helps more than a command.

Do Jack Russell Terriers bark a lot?

They are a vocal breed and they notice everything, so they bark more readily than many dogs. In our experience it is not random: our Jack Russell barks at passers-by, the doorbell, loud sounds outside, thunder and wind, and never at nothing.
